# Market Positioning

## Product Wedge

VishwaVaani should not compete as another general-purpose “AI English tutor.” Its wedge is travel readiness for Indian learners who know basic English but struggle during live, high-pressure conversations.

The product promise is:

> Help Indian learners speak confidently and complete real travel tasks abroad.

This positioning combines:

- mission-based practice for airports, hotels, restaurants, directions, and common problems;
- repair-language training for repetition, clarification, and confirmation;
- intelligibility coaching without accent erasure;
- Indian names, addresses, diets, destinations, and learner contexts;
- exposure to varied speeds and international accents.

## Initial Audience

The primary audience is an A2-to-B1 learner preparing for international travel, study, or early-career mobility. The learner can often read English and form simple sentences but freezes, misses natural-speed questions, or cannot recover from misunderstanding.

The first release should prioritize first-time international travelers, students, and young professionals. Broader school, workplace, and institutional use cases can follow after the consumer learning loop is proven.

## Competitive Frame

Established products validate demand for AI conversation, speech feedback, transcripts, and structured courses. VishwaVaani differentiates through task depth and India-specific personalization rather than through voice technology alone.

The central success question is not “Did the learner finish a lesson?” It is “Can the learner complete the exchange independently, understand follow-ups, and recover when communication breaks down?”

## Commercial Direction

The recommended launch model is freemium:

- free daily speaking practice;
- premium Yatra missions and Uccharan analysis;
- later destination packs and institutional reporting.

Go-to-market messaging should emphasize confidence abroad, realistic speaking practice, and clarity without accent loss. Early channels include study-abroad and travel communities, Indian-language creators, campuses, and young-professional cohorts.
