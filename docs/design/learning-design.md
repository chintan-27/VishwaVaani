# Learning Design

## Learning Goal

The initial target is functional spoken independence from high A2 to B1. Learners should complete common travel exchanges, understand routine follow-ups, and recover from misunderstandings. Task completion and independence matter more than vocabulary totals or time spent.

## Curriculum Model

Organize learning around missions rather than grammar chapters. Mission clusters include:

- airport and border;
- arrival and accommodation;
- food, shopping, and payment;
- social basics;
- problem solving.

Each scenario progresses through:

> listen → repeat → respond with help → respond independently → handle variation → handle pressure

The scenario engine should constrain the AI with roles, required information, success criteria, allowed assistance, controlled branches, and explicit completion rules. Variation belongs inside this structure; the model should not invent the curriculum during a live session.

## Repair Language

Clarification is a core skill, not a fallback. Teach and score phrases such as:

- “Could you repeat that?”
- “Please speak more slowly.”
- “Do you mean …?”
- “Let me confirm …”

A learner who recovers appropriately should receive credit even when the first exchange fails.

## Pronunciation and Listening

Uccharan optimizes for intelligibility and comprehensibility, not imitation of an American or British accent. Prioritize word stress, sentence rhythm, thought groups, high-impact sound confusions, names, dates, addresses, and connected speech.

Use light cues during speaking and richer post-session feedback. Recommended activities include minimal pairs, shadowing, chunk repetition, accent-comprehension practice, and controlled background-noise practice.

## Evidence of Progress

Track readiness across task completion, comprehension, independence, fluency, clarity, meaning-preserving grammar, and self-reported confidence. Progress requires fewer hints and successful performance across variations—not merely repeating the same script.
